<h1 class="article-title">Top Attractions in Vernal, Utah</h1>

<div class="article-body">

<div class="article-summary-box">Vernal packs more variety into a small radius than almost anywhere in Utah — world-class dinosaur fossils, ancient rock art, canyon-country backcountry, and state parks with prehistoric trackways, all within 30 minutes of downtown. Here are the attractions worth planning your trip around.</div>

## Guided Backcountry UTV Tours

Adventure Tours Vernal runs guided Kawasaki KRX 1000 tours across five trail systems surrounding the Uintah Basin. Each trail delivers different terrain and history — sandy desert washes at Doc's Beach, red rock canyons at Moonshine Arch, dramatic gorge corridors at Ashley Gorge, outlaw hideout country on Outlaw Trail, and panoramic ridge views at Asphalt Ridge. Tours include stops at Fremont and Ute petroglyphs, natural arches, and historic Wild West landmarks. Browse all [guided UTV trail tours](/utv/) or [check dates and book online](/booking/).

Dave and Trudy Wilson guide every tour personally. Three hours, $349 per machine (up to 2 riders), all gear included. It's the single most immersive way to experience what makes Vernal's landscape unique.

## Dinosaur National Monument

The Quarry Exhibit Hall displays over 1,500 dinosaur fossils still embedded in the cliff face — Allosaurus, Stegosaurus, Diplodocus, and more from the Jurassic era. Beyond the quarry, the monument stretches across 210,000 acres of canyon country along the Green and Yampa rivers with petroglyphs, scenic drives, and backcountry hiking. Twenty minutes east of downtown Vernal. See our complete [guide to visiting Dinosaur National Monument](/dinosaur-national-monument/visiting-dinosaur-national-monument/) for hours, fees, and the best time to go.

## McConkie Ranch Petroglyphs

Dry Fork Canyon north of Vernal holds some of the most impressive Fremont culture rock art in the American West. The petroglyphs at McConkie Ranch are large, detailed, and visible from a maintained trail along the canyon wall. Free to visit, roughly an hour to see the main panels. One of the most photographed sites in the Uintah Basin. Learn more in our [guide to petroglyphs and rock art near Vernal](/dinosaur-national-monument/petroglyphs-rock-art-vernal/).

## Red Fleet State Park

Red Fleet Reservoir combines swimming, boating, and fishing with a unique draw — dinosaur trackways preserved in the sandstone along the north shore. The three-toed theropod tracks are accessible via a short hike and visible year-round. The red rock setting around the reservoir is dramatic enough on its own. Fifteen minutes from town. See the [Ultimate Guide to Red Fleet State Park](/guides/ultimate-guide-to-red-fleet-state-park/) for trackway access, camping, and boating.

## Utah Field House of Natural History

Downtown Vernal's natural history museum features an outdoor Dinosaur Garden with life-size replicas, plus indoor exhibits covering the geology, paleontology, and cultural history of the Uintah Basin. Good for families and a solid rainy-day option. Typically takes one to two hours.

## Steinaker State Park & Flaming Gorge

Steinaker Reservoir is five minutes from town — swimming, paddleboarding, camping, and easy lakeside walks. For a bigger excursion, Flaming Gorge (about an hour north) is one of Utah's top fishing destinations with dramatic red canyon scenery, lake trout, and kokanee salmon. The drive alone through the Uinta Mountains is worth the trip. Read the [Ultimate Guide to Steinaker State Park](/guides/ultimate-guide-to-steinaker-state-park/) and the [Ultimate Guide to Flaming Gorge](/guides/ultimate-guide-to-flaming-gorge/) to plan either trip.
