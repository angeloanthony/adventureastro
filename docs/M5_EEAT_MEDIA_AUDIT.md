# M5.0 — EEAT & Original Media Audit

**Adventure Tours Vernal · Dinosaur Country content library**
Prepared: July 2026 · Scope: analysis & documentation only (architecture frozen — no code, content, schema, routing, or component changes)

This audit inventories the entire content library and identifies the highest-impact opportunities to strengthen **Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trust (EEAT)** using **original assets** — photography, video, maps, and graphics captured/produced by Dave & Trudy — rather than by writing more articles. The written library is already deep; the missing layer is *proof of first-hand experience*, and that layer is almost entirely made of original media.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The site has **~60 major indexable pages** across nine clusters (destination guides, Dinosaur National Monument, scenic drives, hiking, fishing, camping, itineraries, UTV/commercial, things-to-do), plus ten hub/pillar cornerstone pages. The **written content is excellent**: every article carries a Quick Answer, Key Takeaways, comparison tables, a multi-question FAQ, dense internal linking, an AEO summary block, and disciplined `VERIFY WITH OFFICIAL SOURCE` markers. On text alone this is already the most thorough resource in its market.

**The single defining weakness is the near-total absence of original media.** Concretely:

- **No content article renders a single inline body image.** A repo-wide check for markdown/embedded images across `src/content/**` returns zero. Every article is text-only.
- **56 of ~60 pages use `/images/logo.webp` as their hero.** The hero is metadata-only (og:image/JSON-LD) per the frozen image architecture, so in practice the flagship pages ship with *no* representative photograph at all. The only exceptions with real imagery are the two DNM pages (`20.webp` hero), `moab-utv-tours` (media-rich), `best-restaurants`, `booking`, and `about`.
- **Not one custom map exists** anywhere on a site whose entire trip-planning thesis is "the region is a wheel — Vernal is the hub." The most map-dependent pages (itineraries, gateway drives, multi-fishery/scenic-drive guides) have no map.
- **Almost no first-hand, dated field observation exists in body copy.** Articles are bylined to Dave but read in a generic "a local guide" voice; the heavy `VERIFY WITH OFFICIAL SOURCE` density reads as second-hand precisely because there is no original photo or field note proving the author stood there.
- **The pages that *do* use media partly undercut EEAT with third-party assets:** `about.astro` embeds six generic destination YouTube clips (not Adventure Tours' own tour footage), and `moab-utv-tours` uses a generic YouTube hero embed that should be confirmed as original.

**The opportunity is unusually clean.** Because competitors and AI-generated content cannot fabricate Dave & Trudy standing on a specific trail, holding a specific fish, or photographing a specific petroglyph at golden hour, original media is the one moat that compounds. The recommended program, in priority order:

1. **A trail/tour photography + video library** (the five named trails, the KRX fleet, Dave & Trudy guiding) — feeds every UTV, commercial, and itinerary page and the money pages (`booking`, `about`).
2. **One custom regional hub-and-spoke map** (Vernal at center, drive-times to every destination) — reusable across the guides, itineraries, and things-to-do clusters.
3. **A per-cluster original hero-photo pass** replacing the logo hero on the ~56 affected pages, prioritized by search value and un-substitutability (Red Fleet dinosaur trackway, Red Canyon Overlook, Cub Creek lizard panel, alpine-lake reflections).
4. **Climate charts** on the weather guide and **elevation/geology diagrams** on the seasonal, scenic-drive, and Kings Peak pages.
5. **Dave & Trudy first-hand field notes** (dated, located generally) added wherever the byline currently outruns the evidence.

## 2. Site-Wide Findings

**F1 — Zero rendered original photography in article bodies.** The most consequential finding. Every one of the ~55 content articles is text-only; the hero is metadata, so the reader sees no photograph. This is the largest EEAT and engagement gap on the site and the highest-leverage thing original media fixes.

**F2 — The logo-as-hero problem (56 pages).** `heroImage: /images/logo.webp` is the near-universal default. Replacing it with one genuine, dated, page-specific field photo per page is the highest-leverage, lowest-cost EEAT win in the library. Sequence by cluster value (below).

**F3 — No custom maps anywhere.** A single branded **regional hub-and-spoke map** (Vernal center; DNM ~20 min, Red Fleet ~15 min, Steinaker ~5–10 min, McConkie ~15–20 min, Flaming Gorge ~1 hr, High Uintas) would serve the Vernal guide, every itinerary, things-to-do, and the gateway pages at once. Route-diagram maps are specifically missing on the pages whose *titles* are the drive (SLC/Denver/Grand Junction weekends, `from/salt-lake-city`).

**F4 — The byline outruns the evidence.** Articles are attributed to Dave but contain little first-hand, dated, specific observation. The `VERIFY WITH OFFICIAL SOURCE` markers are editorially correct, but with no original photo or field note beside them the pages read as researched, not lived. Original media + short dated "field note" captions convert the byline from a claim into proof.

**F5 — Third-party media on the EEAT anchor pages.** `about.astro` (the strongest EEAT page — Wilson family photo, Person schema, 5.0★/82 reviews) leans on six generic destination YouTube embeds; `moab-utv-tours` uses a generic YouTube hero. Swapping these for original Adventure Tours footage is a refinement that removes a live contradiction between the schema's first-hand authority and the on-page evidence.

**F6 — The rarest, most un-substitutable assets are un-shot.** The **Red Fleet Early-Jurassic dinosaur trackway** (reachable by paddle), the **Cub Creek Fremont lizard panel**, **McConkie Ranch petroglyphs**, and the **Wall of Bones** are genuine differentiators no competitor or AI can replicate — and none is photographed. These should lead the capture schedule.

**F7 — The weather guide has no charts.** A weather guide with zero climate data-visualization is a glaring, cheap-to-fix gap. Monthly temperature-band and precipitation charts, a UV-by-elevation graphic, and a daily-swing curve would add authority and shareability right next to an already-converting "when to book" CTA. (Build with the `dataviz` skill.)

**F8 — Two mislabeled internal links (flag only — do not edit).** During the read, two CTA links were found pointing to the wrong target: in `dinosaur-national-monument/visiting-dinosaur-national-monument.mdx` a "guided UTV tour" anchor points to `/dinosaur-national-monument/` instead of `/utv/` or `/booking/`; in `dinosaur-national-monument/petroglyphs-rock-art-vernal.mdx` a "guided UTV trail tours" link points to bare `/`. Logged here for the owner; **no change made** (content is frozen).

**F9 — Sibling UTV pages don't cross-link.** The four UTV articles (best-trails, rentals, group, backcountry) and the two things-to-do pages largely fail to link *to each other*; several have no in-body `/booking/` button (only a phone number in the FAQ/summary). A non-salesy internal-linking + CTA pass is a pure commercial win.

**F10 — A few content-hygiene notes.** `what-to-bring.mdx` is the only guide with **no FAQ block and no `author` field** (EEAT gap on an otherwise best-in-class page). `wildlife-hiking-guide` references bird-watching and photography as "future spokes" that now exist and should be linked. `alpine-lakes` references a forthcoming backpacking guide that now exists. Restaurant card images reuse a shared generic asset (`95a.webp`) for the hero and a card.
