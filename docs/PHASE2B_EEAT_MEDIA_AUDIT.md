## 1. The EEAT thesis (what an AI competitor cannot copy)

The written library is best-in-market and, increasingly, so is the cartography — but text and
schematic maps are the layers a well-funded AI-content competitor can most nearly match. The
**moat that compounds** is what only Dave & Trudy can produce:

1. **Original photographs** of specific trails, fish, fossils, and light — proof they stood there.
2. **Dated first-hand field notes and local tips** — lived Experience beside the researched facts.
3. **On-camera guiding** — the literal demonstration of Expertise and Trust.

Everything below is prioritized by how much it strengthens that moat per unit of effort, and —
per the mandate — recommends **only assets Adventure Tours Vernal could realistically create
from its own trips, knowledge, and archive.** No specific facts, quotes, or figures are invented
here; each row proposes an asset *type* and its subject, to be filled from real experience.

## 4. Sequencing & Guardrails

- **Do the desk moat first.** Quick-Win field notes + existing-photo body deployment + charts
  land the fastest EEAT gains with **zero travel** and no dependency — start there.
- **Field work follows the M5 batching** (six field days, season-gated: Day 6 UTV/town first for
  the money pages; Day 4 hiking in summer; Day 5 scenic in fall). This audit adds no new field
  logistics — it re-prioritizes toward the four all-logo hubs (hiking/fishing/camping/scenic) that
  M5 predates the deployment of.
- **Realistic-only.** Every asset is something Dave & Trudy can shoot, write, or draw from their
  own trips, archive, and knowledge. **Invent nothing** — field notes, tips, and captions are
  filled from real experience or left for owner input (as the existing `VERIFY WITH OFFICIAL
  SOURCE` and author-photo/bio TODOs already model).
- **Respect the frozen rails.** Deployment (a later phase) is content/asset work only: heroes and
  body images go through `astro:assets`, stay ≤500 KB webp/avif per the validator, and touch no
  routing/layout/schema/collection/build code. Remote rock-art locations stay deliberately vague.
